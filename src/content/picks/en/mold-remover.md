Bathroom mold does not scrub off easily, because it **roots into caulk and grout**. So when choosing a mold remover, **how long it stays in contact** matters more than how strong it is.

## 1. Ingredient: mostly chlorine

Most bathroom mold removers contain **sodium hypochlorite (chlorine)**, the same family as household bleach. It breaks down mold pigment and disinfects powerfully, but it smells strong and **releases toxic gas when mixed with acidic cleaners**. Check the label for "contains chlorine" and "do not mix."

## 2. Form: gel vs spray

| | Gel | Spray |
|---|---|---|
| Pros | Does not run; stays in contact | Covers large areas fast |
| Cons | Slow for large areas | Runs on vertical surfaces; fumes spread |
| Best for | Caulk lines, grout | Tile walls, ceiling corners, shower enclosures |

**If you buy just one, choose a gel.** Most bathroom mold problems are on sink and bathtub caulk lines. If you choose a spray, foaming versions stay longer on vertical walls.

## 3. Use it safely

- **Ventilate first.** Open the door and turn on the fan before you start.
- **Gloves and mask.** Protect your skin and lungs.
- **No acids on the same day.** Use citric acid, vinegar or acidic toilet cleaners on a different day.
- **Move colored items.** Splashes bleach bath mats and towels.
