Walk down the cleaning aisle and you will find cleaners for bathrooms, kitchens, glass and mold. But most household cleaning can be handled with **three powders**. Each has a different chemistry, so together they cover almost every type of grime.

## What each one does

| Cleaner | Chemistry | Tackles |
|---|---|---|
| Baking soda | Mildly alkaline | Grease, burnt residue, odors |
| Citric acid | Acidic | Limescale, hard water, urine scale |
| Oxygen bleach | Alkaline + oxygen | Mold, grime, yellow stains |

For how each works and where to avoid them, see [baking soda, citric acid and oxygen bleach compared](/en/magazine/cleaning-agents-guide/).

## How to choose

**1. Size: start with 1–2 kg (2–4 lb)**
Bulk bags are cheaper, but powders clump when they absorb moisture. Start with a small bag, see how much you use and scale up. Oxygen bleach is used in large amounts for washer cleaning, so a bigger bag makes sense.

**2. Food-grade vs cleaning-grade**
Baking soda and citric acid come in food-grade versions too. Cleaning-grade is enough and cheaper. If you prefer, use food-grade for things that touch food, like kettles.

**3. Storage: airtight jars, not the bag**
Open bags absorb moisture and cake. Decant into airtight containers. All three are white powders, so **label each jar clearly** to stay safe.

## Buy one more thing separately

There is one job these three cannot do: **black mold rooted in caulk and grout**. That needs a chlorine mold remover. See [how to choose a mold remover](/en/picks/mold-remover/).
