## Where does the smell come from?

Sink odor usually comes from two places. The first is **food and grease film on the strainer and drain opening**, where bacteria grow and create a sour, musty smell. The second is **sewer gas rising from the pipe**. A U-shaped bend under the sink (the trap) holds water that blocks this gas; if there is no trap, or it dries out, the smell comes straight up.

The method above solves the first problem. If the smell continues after cleaning, look at the pipe under your sink. If it runs straight down with no bend, adding an odor trap or calling a plumber is the quickest fix.

## Do baking soda and vinegar need to be used together?

The fizzing itself does not add much cleaning power, because the alkali and acid neutralize each other. In a narrow drain, though, the foam physically helps lift residue off the walls, which is the one case where using them together makes sense. See [baking soda, citric acid and oxygen bleach compared](/en/magazine/cleaning-agents-guide/) for the details.

## FAQ

**My drain is slow to empty.**
This method is for odor. If water pools in the sink, try pulling debris out from below the strainer with a long brush; if that fails, use a plunger or call a plumber.
