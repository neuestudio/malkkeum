    body: The two metal strips at the front inside the drum sense whether clothes are dry. Softener residue makes the cycle end early. Wipe them with a well-wrung damp cloth, then a dry one, and clean lint off the rubber door seal.

    body: If no drain hose is connected, empty the tank after every load. Standing water smells and builds scale. Rinse and dry the tank once a month.

## Why clothes stay damp

A dryer blows hot air through the drum and **recirculates it through the lint filter**. A clogged filter restricts airflow, so **drying takes longer and uses more power**. Softener film on the moisture sensor makes the dryer think clothes are dry while they are still damp. These two causes explain most damp loads.

## Where to look first

| Symptom | Check first |
|---|---|
| Cycles take longer than before | Clogged lint filter, dusty condenser |
| Ends quickly but clothes are damp | Film on the moisture sensor |
| Musty smell after drying | Standing water in the tank, dusty door seal |
| Filter warning comes on often | Clogged mesh (needs washing) |

## Why lint buildup is dangerous

Dryer lint is made of fine fibers that **ignite easily**. If the filter is not emptied, lint can pass inside and settle near hot parts. Most dryer fires start with **a neglected filter**, so emptying it every time is the surest prevention.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use dryer sheets?**
You can, but their softening agents coat the sensor and filter. If you use them, clean both more often.

**The filter has a hole. Can I keep using it?**
Lint will get inside through the hole. Replace it with the same model filter from the manufacturer.
