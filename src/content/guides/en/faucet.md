  - title: Spray with citric acid
    body: Dissolve 1 tsp of citric acid in 200 ml (3/4 cup) of water in a spray bottle and spray the whole faucet, especially the body and the underside of the handle where white scale builds up.
  - title: Wrap with paper towels
    body: Wrap the faucet in paper towels and spray again so they cling. Leave for about 30 minutes to dissolve the hardened scale.
  - title: Clean the aerator
    body: Unscrew the tip of the spout where the water comes out to find a small mesh aerator. Soak it in the citric acid solution, then brush it with a toothbrush. The flow will even out again.
  - title: Scrub the joints
    body: Remove the paper towels and scrub the base where the faucet meets the sink and the gaps around the handle. Black grime usually hides here.

    body: Rinse off the citric acid, then dry the faucet completely with a microfiber cloth. Leftover water creates new spots, so this final dry wipe matters most.

## Limescale and black grime

White marks on a faucet are minerals from tap water, and they dissolve in citric acid. **Black or pink grime** around the base is mold and bacteria growing on the moisture. For that, scrub with dish soap first, and if it is stubborn, treat it separately with a chlorine cleaner on another day.

## Kitchen faucets have grease too

Kitchen faucets often have a layer of grease on top of the limescale. Wipe the grease off with a soapy sponge before spraying citric acid, so the acid reaches the scale directly and works much faster.

## FAQ

**The aerator will not unscrew.**
If it has not been cleaned in a long time, it may be cemented in place by scale. Wrap the tip in a citric-acid paper towel for 30 minutes, then grip it with a rubber glove so your hand does not slip.
