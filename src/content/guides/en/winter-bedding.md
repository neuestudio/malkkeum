  - Take duvets labeled do not wash (wool and some cotton-filled ones) to a dry cleaner.
  - Only wash at 60°C or above if the label allows. High heat can shrink or damage the fabric.

## Why wash it again when you take it out?

Even a duvet washed before storage absorbs **moisture** after six months in a cupboard or vacuum bag, and dust mites multiply on the skin flakes left inside. That is why it smells musty and makes your nose itch when you use it straight away. Wash it once and dry it fully before the first cold night.

## How to wash each filling

| Filling | Machine wash at home | How to wash | Drying |
|---|---|---|---|
| Microfiber, cotton cover | Usually yes | Bedding cycle, 60°C+ if the label allows | Dryer or air dry |
| Duck or goose down | Yes, if the label allows | Around 30°C, mild detergent or wool wash, no softener | Dryer with 2–3 tennis balls, fully |
| Wool | Usually dry clean | Wool cycle only if the label allows | Flat, in the shade |
| Cotton-filled | Usually dry clean | Wet cotton batting clumps | In the sun, turning often |

This table shows the usual case. **The label on each duvet is what counts**, so always check it first.

## Three ways to reduce dust mites

1. **Wash temperature.** Wash duvets and covers at 60°C or above when the label allows.
2. **Vacuuming.** Regularly vacuum duvets you cannot wash, and your mattress, with a bed vacuum or upholstery tool. Mattress care is covered in [Mattress Stains and Dust Mites](/en/guides/mattress/).
3. **Humidity.** Keep the bedroom at 40–60% humidity, and pull the duvet back in the morning instead of making the bed straight away.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does sunlight kill dust mites?**
Sunlight helps dry out moisture, but sunlight alone is not enough to get rid of dust mites. Wash and vacuum as well.

**Do I need to wash a duvet stored in a vacuum bag?**
If it was completely dry when you packed it, spreading it out for a day may be enough. If it smells or feels damp, wash it.
