## Check the filter material first

Hood filters are usually **stainless steel** or **aluminum**. A magnet tells them apart: if it sticks, it is most likely stainless; if not and the filter feels light, it is probably aluminum. Stainless can be soaked in baking soda or oxygen bleach water, but aluminum reacts to alkalis and turns black, so use dish soap only.

## For thick, old grease

Grease that has built up for years will not come off in one go. Repeat the soak and scrub two or three times, or, for a stainless filter, simmer it with water and baking soda in a large pot for 10 minutes. If it still will not budge, buying a new filter saves time and cleaner.

## FAQ

**Can I put it in the dishwasher?**
Only stainless filters that the manufacturer says are dishwasher-safe. Aluminum filters discolor in the dishwasher too.
