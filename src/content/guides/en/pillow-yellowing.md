steps:
  - title: Check the label for fill and washability
    body: Check the pillow label for the fill and wash symbol. Most polyester fiber pillows are washable, down needs a wool cycle or dry cleaner, and memory foam, latex and buckwheat pillows must not be soaked. For those, wash only the case and follow the no-wash method below.
  - title: Remove the case and pretreat
    body: Wash the case separately. Yellowing is usually in the middle where your head rests. Rub a little mild detergent into the worst spots to help the soak work.
  - title: Soak in oxygen bleach
    body: Fill a basin or tub with 40–50°C water and fully dissolve 2–3 tablespoons of sodium percarbonate. Wearing gloves, press the pillow down to push the air out and let the water soak in, then leave it for 30 minutes to an hour. Use this only on white polyester pillows.
  - title: Machine wash
    body: Press out the water, put the pillow in a large laundry bag and machine wash. Use less detergent than usual and add an extra rinse. Wash two pillows together or add towels so the drum stays balanced.
  - title: Dry it all the way through
    body: After spinning, pat the fill back into shape and dry in a well-ventilated spot for about two days, turning and patting it now and then. A damp core grows mold and smells, so dry until it no longer feels cool inside.
  - title: For pillows you cannot wash
    body: Dab yellow spots on memory foam or latex with a well-wrung damp towel and dry in the shade, since direct sun makes the foam crumble. Air buckwheat pillows in the sun as they are, and replace the hulls if they break down or smell.
cautions:
  - Do not use oxygen bleach on down, wool, silk or colored fabric. It can damage fibers or remove color.
  - Never mix oxygen bleach with chlorine bleach or vinegar.
  - Never put memory foam or latex pillows in a washer or dryer. They tear or crumble.
  - Do not use a pillow that is not fully dry. It invites mold and dust mites.
tips:
  - A thin pillow protector under the case greatly slows yellowing.
  - Drying your hair before bed reduces the moisture and oil that soak in.
  - If yellowing stays after washing and the fill has gone flat, replace it. Fiber pillows typically last 1–2 years, memory foam 2–3.

## Why do pillows turn yellow?

While you sleep, **sweat and skin oil** pass through the case into the pillow. They **oxidize and turn yellow** over time. If you wash only the case, a pillow can yellow within months. Even in winter, thick bedding and heating keep the oil building up.

## Can your pillow be washed?

| Fill | Wash the pillow? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Polyester fiber | Yes | Oxygen bleach soak, machine wash, dry two days |
| Down | Check label | Wool cycle with wool wash, or dry cleaner |
| Memory foam, latex | No | Dab with a damp towel, dry in shade |
| Buckwheat | No | Air in the sun, replace broken hulls |
| Orthopedic | Check label | Follow the manufacturer |

## Frequently asked questions

**What if the yellow will not come out?**
Old oxidized stains may not lift in one go. Try a second soak. If some remains, it is not a hygiene problem, but consider replacing the pillow.

**Can pillows go in the dryer?**
Polyester pillows can be tumble dried on low if the label allows. Never dry memory foam or latex.
