  - title: Wash with dish soap first
    body: Whatever the material, wash off food and grease with dish soap first. Move the sponge along the knife marks to clear out residue caught in the grooves.
  - title: Soak plastic boards in oxygen bleach
    body: Dissolve 2 tbsp of sodium percarbonate in warm water at 40–60°C (104–140°F) and soak the board for 30 minutes. Red and yellow stains from chili or turmeric fade, and the board is sanitized at the same time.
  - title: Scrub wooden boards with salt and lemon
    body: Never soak wooden boards, or they will crack. Sprinkle coarse salt over the surface and scrub firmly with the cut side of half a lemon. The salt acts as an abrasive and the lemon removes odors.
  - title: Rinse and dry standing up
    body: Rinse well, wipe off the water and stand the board upright somewhere with airflow. Lying flat traps moisture underneath and invites mold.
  - title: Oil wooden boards
    body: Once a month, when a wooden board is completely dry, rub in a thin layer of food-safe board oil or mineral oil. Oiled wood absorbs less water, so it stains and cracks less.
cautions:
  - Keep wooden boards out of the dishwasher, boiling water and long soaks. They warp and split.
  - Oxygen bleach irritates skin, so wear rubber gloves.
  - Use separate boards for raw meat and vegetables, or wash the board with soap immediately after cutting meat.
tips:
  - If deep knife grooves stay dark even after cleaning, bacteria can hide there. It is time for a new board.
  - Fishy smells come out easily with rice-rinsing water or diluted vinegar.
  - Wetting the board with cold water before use helps stop colors and smells from soaking in.

## Why cutting boards need extra care

A cutting board gets new tiny grooves every day from your knife. Moisture and food left in those grooves create an ideal place for bacteria to grow. So in addition to washing after every use, it is worth **sanitizing once a week**.

## Care by material

| Material | Washing | Sanitizing and stains | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plastic | Dish soap | Oxygen bleach soak, boiling water | Storing near heat |
| Wood | Dish soap, quickly | Salt and lemon, diluted vinegar | Soaking, dishwasher |
| Silicone / TPU | Dish soap | Boiling water, oxygen bleach | Harsh scouring pads |

## FAQ

**Can I use chlorine bleach?**
For plastic boards, a short soak at the dilution on the label is fine. Oxygen bleach is easier at home because it smells less and rinses off easily. If you do use chlorine bleach, rinse thoroughly so it never meets vinegar or citric acid.
