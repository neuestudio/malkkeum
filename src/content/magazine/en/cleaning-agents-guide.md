Baking soda, citric acid and oxygen bleach appear in almost every cleaning tip. They are all white powders and easy to mix up, but **once you know their chemistry, where to use each becomes obvious.** It comes down to one sentence:

> Clean grime with its opposite.

Grease and body oils are **acidic**, so alkaline cleaners remove them. Limescale and hard water are **alkaline**, so acidic cleaners remove them.

## At a glance

| | Baking soda | Citric acid | Oxygen bleach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chemistry | Mildly alkaline | Acidic | Alkaline + oxygen bleach |
| Removes | Grease, burnt residue, odors | Limescale, hard water, urine scale | Mold, grime, yellow stains |
| Typical use | Pots, cooktops, drains | Faucets, kettles, shower heads, toilets | Washer drums, cloths, cutting boards |
| How | Powder, paste or solution | Dissolved and sprayed | Dissolved in water above 40°C (104°F), soak |
| Avoid | Aluminum (discolors) | Marble, cement grout, iron | Wool, silk, aluminum, jewelry |

## Baking soda: the gentle all-rounder

Its fine particles make it **a gentle abrasive that rarely scratches**, and it absorbs odors too. Its cleaning power is modest, so it has limits on heavy grime, but it is the mildest and safest for everyday use.

→ [Cleaning a burnt stainless steel pot](/en/guides/burnt-pot/), [getting rid of shoe cabinet odor](/en/guides/shoe-cabinet/)

## Citric acid: the white-mark specialist

It works best on **white limescale and hard water spots** from tap water minerals. It is odorless, which makes it a good replacement for vinegar. Being an acid, it etches marble and cement grout and can rust iron.

→ [Descaling an electric kettle](/en/guides/electric-kettle/), [making a faucet shine](/en/guides/faucet/)

## Oxygen bleach: the power of oxygen

When dissolved, it releases oxygen that **bleaches and disinfects**. It barely dissolves in cold water, so use **water at 40–60°C (104–140°F)**. It is strongly alkaline and irritates skin, so always wear gloves. Never store the dissolved solution in a sealed container; the oxygen builds up and the lid can pop off. Mix only what you need.

→ [Cleaning a top-load washer](/en/guides/top-loader/), [removing stains from cutting boards](/en/guides/cutting-board/)

## Is mixing them better?

**Baking soda + citric acid** fizzes dramatically, but the alkali and acid neutralize each other and both lose power. Unless you need the foam's physical action, as in a drain, use them **separately, one after the other**.

Above all, **never use citric acid with bleach.** Mixing them releases toxic chlorine gas. Read [cleaning products you should never mix](/en/magazine/never-mix-cleaners/).
